# Taking a short briefing

Use this when the consultant gives a role in a few lines instead of a job description: a title and a client, a forwarded note, a spoken request. Whatever the briefing leaves open, polyseek has to assume, and an assumption that is wrong costs a plan correction or a whole search run.

## Ask once

Send one message with a short numbered list. Ask only for the points the consultant has not covered, in their language, and say that "open" or "don't know" is a valid answer. Do not ask a second round; what stays open becomes an assumption or an open question of the plan, which the consultant sees and can correct for free.

| Point | Why it changes the result |
| --- | --- |
| The client of the mandate | Its employees are excluded, its competitors become target companies, its size frames the role. |
| The role: title, level, reporting line, scope (team, budget, sites, revenue) | Decides which role levels fit fully and which only as reserve. |
| The must-haves, apart from the nice-to-haves, and how a broad one is judged ("one of the three suffices") | Every candidate is scored and verified against the must-haves. Many must-haves thin the market; ask which ones are really binding. |
| The domain the candidate must come from: industry, product, customer segment | It anchors the search. Without one the plan comes back with an open question. |
| Region, work model, relocation | Without a region the plan has to assume one. |
| Target companies the consultant already has in mind | Every named company is searched as a target company. |
| A reference profile: a person or a described profile that is exactly right | Translated into preferred traits, titles and origin areas. |
| Off-limits companies and people | Never delivered. |
| Candidates the client already knows | Excluded and reported by name. |
| Hard limits the client set: years of experience, team size, compensation band, start date | polyseek sets no numeric limit on its own. |
| The depth | basic for a first read of the market, standard for most mandates, premium for niche roles and thin markets. |

## Pass it on

- Write the role, the must-haves, the domain, the region and the limits as one clear text in the consultant's words and pass it as the input. If the input is a job description or a URL, pass the consultant's additions as the briefing instead.
- Named target companies, the reference profile and the way a criterion is judged go into the briefing.
- The client, the known candidates and the off-limits lists have fields of their own; use them.
- Keep the consultant's wording for must-haves. Never turn a nice-to-have into a must-have, never add a requirement, a number or a company the consultant did not give, and never leave one out.
- A wish that touches age, gender, origin, religion, disability, sexual orientation or marital status is passed as the consultant said it; polyseek translates it into a job-related criterion and the plan shows the translation.

## If the consultant wants to start right away

Start. Say in one sentence which of the points above the plan will have to assume, and point them out again when you show the plan.
